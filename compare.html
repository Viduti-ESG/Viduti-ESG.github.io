How to choose an
ESG data provider in India

Choosing an ESG data or BRSR compliance platform is mostly a question of fit, not features. This guide sets out the criteria that actually separate providers, the questions to put to any vendor, and how to run a trial that tells you something useful.

We deliberately don’t publish other providers’ pricing or capability claims here. Their commercial terms aren’t public and second-hand figures go stale fast — ask each vendor directly, and hold them to the same criteria below.

Seven criteria that actually matter

1. Data provenance
Is every data point traceable to a source document you can open — a filing, a disclosure, a regulator’s database — or is it a modelled estimate? Ask which of the two you are buying, and what share is each.
Ask: show me the source for this number
2. Methodology transparency
Can you reproduce a score from the published methodology? If the weights, normalisation and peer set are not documented, you cannot defend the number to a board or an assurer.
Ask: can I recompute this myself?
3. Coverage where you need it
Headline company counts hide the detail. What matters is coverage of your universe — your sector, your market-cap band, your suppliers. Ask for a coverage check against a list you supply.
Ask: how many of my 40 names do you cover?
4. Regulatory fit
A platform built for one disclosure regime rarely maps cleanly onto another. If your obligation is SEBI BRSR, check whether the data model is BRSR-native or mapped across from a different framework — and where that mapping loses fidelity.
Ask: which regime is this built on?
5. Update cadence
Disclosure data ages on an annual cycle, but regulation moves quarterly. Ask when the dataset was last refreshed, what triggers a refresh, and how you find out that something changed.
Ask: what is the date on this data?
6. Export and lock-in
Can you get your data and your work out in a usable format if you leave? Check export formats, API access, and whether analysis you build inside the tool is portable.
Ask: what does my exit look like?
7. Total cost of ownership
List price is rarely the real number. Count seats, refresh fees, onboarding, and the analyst hours needed to make the output usable. A cheaper tool that needs two days of cleanup a month is not cheaper.
Ask: what will this cost in year two?

How to run a trial that tells you something

Bring your own list
Give every vendor the same 20–40 companies you actually care about. A demo on the vendor’s hand-picked examples tells you nothing about coverage of your universe.
Chase one number to its source
Pick a single figure — one company’s Scope 1 emissions, say — and follow it back to the filing it came from. How hard that is tells you more than any feature list.
Produce one real deliverable
Make something you would actually send — a board slide, a peer benchmark, a gap analysis — and count the manual steps. That count is your real running cost.
Check a company you know well
Run a company whose disclosures you already understand. Errors you would never catch on an unfamiliar name are obvious on a familiar one.
